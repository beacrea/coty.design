# Why Coty Beasley Is a Strong Candidate for Perplexity’s Design Systems Lead Role

## Executive assessment

Coty Beasley is an unusually strong candidate because the role is not merely asking for a design-system manager or a component-library designer. Perplexity wants a hands-on design-engineering leader who can establish semantic infrastructure, own its GitHub lifecycle, govern contributions, preserve Figma–code coherence, make accessibility and theming systemic, and invent an AI-native operating model. Beasley has already done those things as one integrated practice rather than as disconnected specialties.[^1][^2]

The strongest positioning is: **a product-minded design-systems architect who can make Perplexity’s visual language explicit, executable, governable, and legible to both people and AI agents.** His evidence spans a four-year production system, a clean-slate semantic rearchitecture, public standards-oriented token infrastructure, and an evidence-based model of design-system capabilities.[^3][^1]

This is not a perfect keyword match. The most material gaps are native iOS/Android pipeline ownership, high-volume governance across many feature teams, production Storybook use, and a broad published React component library. Those gaps are real, but they are narrower than the core fit and can be addressed directly rather than obscured.[^1]

## Distinctive advantages

### Systems depth

Beasley approaches a design system as infrastructure: semantic vocabulary, resolution rules, package boundaries, component contracts, release mechanics, governance, documentation, and adoption form one coherent product. That orientation fits Perplexity’s need for a foundation spanning web, iOS, Android, macOS, and Windows rather than a collection of visually similar components.[^5][^3]

His model also separates invariant semantics from platform adapters. That is the right architectural instinct for Perplexity: common intent should stay stable while platform-specific interaction, rendering, and accessibility behavior can vary without fragmenting the language.[^5][^1]

### AI credibility

Beasley’s AI relevance is structural, not fashionable. His design-system work includes agent-legible manifests, machine-readable rules, schemas, validators, knowledge mirrors, workstream event logs, and governance for agent-authored contributions. His broader professional identity also combines product architecture, AI innovation, and design engineering, which aligns with the career through-line presented on his public portfolio.[^4][^6][^1]

That background is especially relevant because Perplexity is expanding from an answer engine into browsers, agents, persistent workflows, and cross-device experiences. Its current products include web and mobile answer experiences, Comet across desktop and mobile, and long-running agentic workflows through Perplexity Computer. A system for that environment must eventually govern not only static UI appearance but also agent states, trust signals, citations, permissions, progress, interruption, handoff, and continuity across surfaces.[^7][^8][^9]

### Product leadership

Beasley is not limited to design-system implementation. His profile includes product and design leadership at Underline and prior leadership at Neighborly and Edge Up Sports, giving him experience connecting system decisions to business priorities, organizational constraints, and product roadmaps. That matters because this lead will evaluate contributions from product teams and balance new capabilities, migrations, and deprecations—not simply approve component styling.[^2][^3]

### Evidence culture

His artifacts show a consistent preference for computed values, explicit provenance, CI enforcement, specifications, ADRs, stable diagnostics, and self-audit. The capability-map work even removed an earlier analytical axis after the evidence failed to support it. This maps closely to Perplexity’s stated values of craftsmanship, ownership, entrepreneurship, scholarship, and partnership.[^10][^11][^4]

### Founder-level ownership

Perplexity says all roles are hands-on and asks applicants to show personal contributions and clear evidence of ownership. Beasley can do this unusually well: the repository history, release record, authored specifications, validators, specimen archive, and open-source infrastructure distinguish what he personally built from what a team merely shipped.[^12][^1]

## The strongest narrative

The most persuasive application story is not “experienced design leader who also codes.” It is:

> **Coty Beasley builds semantic infrastructure that keeps products coherent as teams, platforms, and AI-assisted contributors multiply. He has owned the full lifecycle—from visual language and token architecture through GitHub governance, release engineering, Figma synchronization, accessibility computation, documentation, and agent-readable workflows.**

Three proof points should carry the application:

- **Production ownership:** Four years, 1,412 commits, approximately 92% personal authorship, 20 releases, five distribution targets, automated CI/release machinery, and governed migrations.[^1]
- **Semantic and visual sophistication:** Two systems built from scratch, role-based token architecture, dual schemes, perceptual color design, computed contrast, responsive behavior, and component-level contracts.[^10][^1]
- **AI-native systems thinking:** Machine-readable governance, agent manifests, deterministic schemas, agent-discoverable documentation, and contribution controls designed for both human and machine collaborators.[^4][^1]

The claim should be framed around Perplexity’s outcome: **feature teams can move faster because system coherence becomes the default path, not because a central team manually reviews every decision.** This directly connects Beasley’s encoded-governance approach to Perplexity’s requirement for speed without fragmentation.[^2]

## Honest gaps

| Gap | Risk | Best framing |
|---|---|---|
| Native mobile pipelines | Underline’s shipped distributions are web-oriented; there is no demonstrated production iOS/Android token pipeline.[^1] | Emphasize the framework-neutral contract and adapter architecture, then state that platform specialists would inform native adapters. Do not imply shipped native experience. |
| Multi-team contribution volume | Governance was built for multi-team participation, but the core systems were operated substantially solo.[^1] | Distinguish governance architecture from governance throughput. Use prior product leadership as evidence of cross-functional judgment, while acknowledging that high-volume inbound stewardship is the step-up. |
| Storybook | Documentation used Supernova, custom archives, and VitePress rather than Storybook in production.[^1] | Treat Storybook as replaceable tooling, not a conceptual gap. Be ready to discuss stories, controls, visual regression, documentation, accessibility tests, and release integration concretely. |
| React component-library breadth | The strongest shipped work is token and specification infrastructure; Underline became token-only and Beacon currently demonstrates a narrower component surface.[^1] | Lead with scalable component API design and contracts, but prepare a focused React/TypeScript implementation artifact showing primitives, tests, keyboard behavior, and visual regression. |
| “Large organizations” wording | The role asks for experience at large organizations, while much of Beasley’s strongest ownership comes from founder-led and venture-backed environments.[^2][^3] | Position this as high-complexity, high-autonomy ownership across regulated, civic, fintech, enterprise, and infrastructure domains—not as Fortune 100 design-system scale. |
| Location model | The posting includes Remote (United States), but Perplexity’s interview guidance says remote candidates are considered case by case and employees near offices are generally expected in person four days weekly.[^2][^12] | State location and travel expectations clearly early in the process. Avoid assuming that the “Remote” label guarantees a fully location-agnostic arrangement. |

None of these should be hidden. Perplexity’s interview guidance explicitly favors precise skill descriptions, practical work samples, hands-on depth, and evidence of personal ownership over buzzwords. Directly naming the boundaries will make the stronger evidence more credible.[^12]

## Application strategy

### Lead artifact order

1. **Underline production system:** Establish real ownership, duration, release discipline, governance, and migration history.[^1]
2. **Beacon archive:** Demonstrate current visual judgment, semantic architecture, responsive behavior, dual schemes, documentation, and agent consumption.[^1]
3. **`dtcg-formulas`:** Prove TypeScript/specification depth and show the forward-looking thesis around diffable, provenance-preserving token computation.[^3][^4]
4. **Capability map:** Demonstrate scholarship, field awareness, evidence quality, and willingness to revise a model when the data does not hold.[^10][^4]

### Interview emphasis

- Describe decisions as trade-offs: why GitHub retained structural authority, why adapters were optional, why dark mode was independently resolved, and why contribution rules became executable checks.
- Separate personal authorship from organizational outcomes with exact numbers and named artifacts; Perplexity explicitly requests this.[^12]
- Demonstrate product-specific thinking by identifying where Perplexity’s growing multi-platform system needs shared semantics versus platform-native divergence. The adjacent Design Systems engineering role confirms five target platforms and a stack centered on React, TypeScript, CSS, and design tokens.[^5]
- Bring a concrete 30/60/90-day hypothesis: audit existing product surfaces and token pipelines; define semantic vocabulary and contribution boundaries; then pilot a thin vertical slice across Figma, React, one native adapter, documentation, accessibility checks, and release automation.
- Prepare a compact React artifact before interviews to neutralize the largest evidentiary gap. It should include a small set of primitives, typed APIs, token consumption, dark mode, keyboard and screen-reader behavior, tests, visual regression, and Storybook or an equivalent inspectable environment.

## Bottom line

Beasley would be excellent because his strongest work sits exactly where this role sits: between visual design and code, semantics and implementation, autonomy and governance, human collaboration and AI-assisted production. Most candidates are likely to be strongest in one or two of those dimensions; his candidacy is differentiated by treating all of them as one versioned, testable system.[^3][^2][^1]

The decisive application move is to make the evidence easy to verify. If Perplexity sees only a senior product/design executive, the fit may be missed; if it sees the repository history, semantic architecture, visual specimens, governance machinery, accessibility computation, and agent-readable workflow as a single body of work, the role alignment is unusually strong.
